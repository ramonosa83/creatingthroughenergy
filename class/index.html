What's actually happening

You can't believe your way into a reality your body still codes as dangerous.

Here's the deal.

Your nervous system's first job is to keep you alive. When it reads a threat, it mobilizes — heart rate up, muscles braced, breath shallow — and then it expects you to do something. Run. Fight. Shake it off. The body is built to discharge that charge physically, and when it does, the system resets.

But you can't run from an invoice. You can't fight a bank balance. So when the threat is more — being seen, charging what you're worth, having more than the people you love — the body mobilizes and never gets to finish. The charge stays in. The system stays armed. And every time you get near the number, it's right there: the tightening, the stomach drop, the quiet, full-body no.

That's not a belief. It's an unfinished stress response your body has been carrying for years. And it decides your ceiling long before your conscious mind gets a vote.

Meditation works from the top down — mind to body. Somatic work goes from the bottom up — the body discharges what it's holding, and the mind follows. You need both. Most people have only been given the first half.

I teach meditation. I'll keep teaching it. It regulates the state beautifully. But regulating a state and completing a stress response are two different things — and the second one is active. You participate in it. You move, you breathe, you make sound, you let the body do the thing it's been trying to do.

That's what somatic work is. And when you do it consistently, the research on vagal tone, heart-rate variability, and recovery time all point the same direction: the baseline shifts. You don't just calm down faster. You start from a calmer place.
